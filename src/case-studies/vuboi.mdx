## the brief

Most designs die in a design file, waiting for an engineering team that's always busy. Mine don't. Vuboi is where I'm proving it: a community-driven wellness product I'm designing end to end — the vision, the flows, the interface, the words — and shipping the first pieces of on my own.

I'm not an engineer, and I don't pretend to be. I'm a designer who refuses to let the work end at a screenshot.

## what's live today

I'm honest about where the product is, because that honesty is part of the craft. Three pieces are real:

- **the marketing page**, the front door: make a stranger understand in seconds what Vuboi is and why it's for them
- **the workout planner** (draft), where the language of the product gets designed: how a plan is structured, how much complexity to show and how much to hide
- **the workout tracker**, the screen you touch mid-set, sweating and distracted, so it's designed for the worst conditions: big targets, no friction, instant feedback

The page that earns attention, the flow that creates a plan, the screen that captures the effort. Everything else is designed and waiting behind them.

## the product behind it

Underneath sits a library of 540 exercises, organised the way a coach thinks: by equipment, body part and difficulty, and by whether a movement is measured in reps, time or distance. On top of it, a real training vocabulary: supersets, circuits, rest, tempo, multi-week programs with a deload week built in.

The training logic isn't guesswork. I'm earning my personal-training certification through NFPT, an NCCA-accredited program, so when Vuboi tells a primary mover from a secondary one, that comes from inside the domain.

## designing for the loneliness

The hardest problem in fitness isn't the exercise. People quit because they're doing it alone. So the heart of Vuboi is the connective tissue: sharing plans, following a coach, encouraging each other. The tracker records not just what you lifted but how you felt before and after, because mood is what decides whether you come back.

## what it's really about

Wellness isn't a heroic transformation. It's small, consistent, shared actions that compound: a workout, a meal, a note of encouragement from someone on the same path. I'm not designing features. I'm designing a shorter distance between a person and their next good decision.
