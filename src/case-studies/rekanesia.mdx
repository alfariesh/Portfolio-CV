## the day a button took a week

In 2021 I was designing Rekan, a point-of-sale app for fertiliser kiosks, and I didn't think about design systems at all. Then the product scaled. One day I was asked to change a single element, and it was brutal: the same component lived in dozens of places, each slightly different, each fixed by hand. A change that should have taken minutes ate days.

A mentor said what I needed to hear: the smoothness you feel now is debt you haven't paid yet. That sentence is why Rekanesia exists.

## earning the right to a system

I didn't start by designing a system. I started by auditing one: every screen I'd shipped, which elements repeated, how often, and how much logic each one carried.

The winner was the stepper, a humble quantity input. It was the most used and most logic-heavy component in the app, with presets tied to real bag sizes like 50 kg and 30 kg. Getting it right meant understanding the business, not just the pixels. A design system isn't a land grab; you earn it one high-leverage component at a time.

## the seven-day rebrand, delivered in two

In 2022 the Ministry of Agriculture noticed Rekan. It was already in the hands of 28,000 kiosks, so it became the natural choice to run the national subsidised-fertiliser program, as iPubers. The catch: rebrand the whole app in seven days, design to release.

Duplicating and recolouring every asset would have been fast to start and impossible to maintain. Rebuilding from scratch was out. So I moved the foundation to variable design tokens and made theming a property of the system instead of a manual repaint. I presented the full rebrand and handed it to development on day two.

## from the design file to production

A design system that lives only in a design file is a suggestion. So one change carries all the way to production: update a variable, sync it through Token Studio, commit to GitHub, build with Style Dictionary, deploy to a CDN. No one re-types values into code. Three principles govern every decision: accessible on small screens and low-end devices, achievable so the right path is the easy one, and scalable as if a fourth, fifth and sixth brand are coming.

## what I learned

The most valuable thing Rekanesia gave me wasn't a component library. It was a way of thinking: naming, layering, versioning, making sure the smallest unit is right so the largest structure holds. One source of truth. Many brands. No rebuilds.
