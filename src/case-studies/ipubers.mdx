## the brief

Indonesia spends roughly Rp 54 trillion a year subsidising fertiliser. PT Pupuk Indonesia delivers it through 516 warehouses, 1,067 distributors and 27,136 kiosks. The problem was never delivering it. It was proving that every sale went to the right farmer, within quota, when that proof was assembled by hand, on paper, weeks after the fact.

iPubers is the rebrand and evolution of Rekan, refocused on one mission: subsidy redemption, done right, at the point of sale. I led the user experience and interface design for the redemption flow, and built the personas from field research with real kiosk operators.

## the counter-intuitive problem

Most people assume the hard part of a national subsidy is logistics. It isn't: the allocation was already delivered at around 95% every year. The expensive failure was administrative. Reporting was manual and the data sat in disconnected systems, so the government corrected billings it couldn't verify, clawing back money for sales that may have been perfectly legitimate.

The number that made everyone stop: Rp 200 billion in subsidy-billing corrections in 2022 alone. Not lost fertiliser. Lost trust.

## one redemption, two and a half minutes

The design challenge was compressing a verification process into something a kiosk owner can finish in about two and a half minutes, even on a weak connection:

1. input the farmer's national ID number
2. check stock against the kiosk's live inventory
3. check the farmer's quota in the ministry's e-Alokasi system
4. photograph the farmer's ID card, geotagged and timestamped
5. redeem: stock and allocation update at once, and the data reaches the government in about 120 ms

For blank-spot kiosks there's an offline mode, and a rule that lets the photo be taken elsewhere in the same sub-district, so a connectivity problem never stops a farmer from getting fertiliser.

The redemption standard was written into national policy, the technical guidelines of the Directorate-General of Agricultural Infrastructure, which made iPubers the official method. Stock is now visible in real time across all 27,066 kiosks, and the old SMS stock monitoring is gone.

## why it matters

Behind the numbers there are two people. The farmer, who needs the right fertiliser at the right moment in a season that doesn't wait. And the kiosk owner, who used to lose four extra working days at the end of every month assembling reports, carrying the quiet stress of "what if my notes are wrong?"

Good governance, it turns out, is a user-experience problem.
